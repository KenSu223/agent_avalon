\section{Counter-schema probes: where the prior re-enters}
\label{sec:controlled}

Benchmark items differ in many ways besides guessability, so the resistant/answerable split cannot isolate the prior's
effect on a given fact. We therefore built an instrument that manipulates congruence within an item.

\paragraph{Items.} Each item is a minimal pair over one slot, stated in passing while the user asks the assistant for
something unrelated: C (schema-congruent: ``big files upload more slowly'') or I (schema-incongruent: ``big files upload
faster''). The two versions differ only in the slot words, with no surprise markers or reasons. Besides rules, the set
contains inverted procedures, unusual but real formatting preferences, knowledge updates (a value stated and later
changed, where the I version changes \emph{away} from the prior), and neutral controls. The items were written by
Claude (an LLM) following written rules and were \emph{measured}, not generated, with other models: \nCand{} candidates
were filtered by pre-registered gates on prior strength (V1: the reader picks the C option with probability $\geq0.8$
with no information, 20 samples, measured on four families) and comprehension (V2: the stated version is reported right
after hearing it, on three families). \nFinal{} pairs passed (\nFinalRule{} rules, \nFinalProc{} procedures,
\nFinalPref{} preferences, \nFinalUpd{} updates, \nFinalNeut{} neutral). Priors are model-specific: among the
\nFinalNonNeutral{} non-neutral items, the strong-prior criterion holds for \strongLlama{} under Llama-3.3-70B,
\strongGptoss{} under gpt-oss-120b and only \strongDeepseek{} under DeepSeek-V3.2, so $g$ must always be measured for the
reader in question. A positive control (serial reproduction chains) reproduces the expected one-directional
regularisation toward the schema at a low rate. A stratified human review of 100 items is pending.

\paragraph{Design.} 192 simulated users each mention eight items (versions counterbalanced) over four sessions,
followed by twelve unrelated distractor sessions screened for leakage. Nine memory systems ingest the sessions; at each
checkpoint two judges code each item in the memory text as retained, dropped or flipped to the other version, and the
agent answers a forced-choice question about it from its own context. Everything is run twice, with Qwen3-235B and with
Llama-3.3-70B as both the compactor and the reader, on the \emph{same} 432 items and histories.

\paragraph{Results (Table~\ref{tab:step4}).} Three findings replicate across both families. (i) \emph{Deletion is blind
to the prior}: the retention gap $\Delta R$ is near zero for every rewrite system (largest $|\Delta R|$ \dRQwenMaxAbs{}
under Qwen, \dRLlamaMaxAbs{} under Llama). (ii) \emph{Compactors do not rewrite facts toward the schema}: $\Delta F$ is at most \dFQwenMax{} (Qwen) and
\dFLlamaMax{} (Llama) for every system. (iii) \emph{The prior re-enters at read time}: when items are deleted, the agent answers
from its prior, so the belief gap grows with the deletion rate (Spearman $\rho\geq0.97$ across systems in both families);
for the product compaction prompts of Codex, Aider and the Anthropic API $\Delta K$ is \dKrwQwenLo--\dKrwQwenHi{} under Qwen and \dKrwLlamaLo--\dKrwLlamaHi{}
under Llama, while their accuracy on congruent items stays near ceiling. System rankings by retention agree across families
($\rho=\rhoRetFam$), although how aggressively a system compacts depends on the backbone (the neutral rewrite deletes
\dropNeutralRollQwen{} of items under Qwen and \dropNeutralRollLlama{} under Llama).

Is deletion prior-directed when the compactor is weak? With an 8B compactor (Llama-3.1-8B), most items are deleted within two
sessions; in the few cells where retention is neither at floor nor at ceiling we find no reliable prior-directed
deletion ($\Delta R=\weakDr$ [\weakDrLo, \weakDrHi], pre-registered). The prior's main route into the score is therefore the
reader, not the writer.

Knowledge updates reveal a sharper version of the effect: when the update moves away from the prior, the agent
reverts to the old, congruent value \updQwen{} (Qwen) and \updLlama{} (Llama) more often than when the update moves toward
the prior; stale memory and prior jointly override the change. Finally, \pcs{} computed from the agents' answers matches
the judge-coded retention closely (mean absolute error \pcsMaeQwen{} and \pcsMaeLlama{}, against \accMaeQwen{} and
\accMaeLlama{} for raw accuracy), which is direct evidence that the correction recovers what the memory kept.

\paragraph{A failed control.} We pre-registered that neutral items would show no belief gap; they did not
($\Delta K=\neutralDkQwen$ and $\neutralDkLlama$). Exploration shows why: once deleted, each neutral item is answered
almost deterministically at $T{=}0$ with the option the model mildly preferred at $T{=}1$, and with only ten such items in
the subset the option labels happen to be unbalanced. The lesson is the one from \S\ref{sec:procedure}: priors must be
measured with the decoding used for scoring, and arbitrary labels should be randomised per user.
