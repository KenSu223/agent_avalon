\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether the loop that keeps agent memory small systematically destroys the content memory
exists for. We built an instrument in which prior-fit is graded, measured on the compactor itself,
and --- in \textsc{E-Shift} --- manipulated directly while the target sentence and its value are held
exactly fixed, and we surrounded it with controls that separate rewriting from capacity pressure,
from retrieval, and from the coder. The protocol, the estimators and the decision rule were frozen on
a development split before any confirmatory family was touched, and we report what the data support
including where they do not. The headline practical result is narrower and sharper than the folk
story: strong compactors do not quietly rewrite your constraints into plausible defaults; under a
binding budget they delete, and \ResultSummarySentence{} The instrument, the stimuli and every
result file are released so the regime boundaries can be pushed further.
