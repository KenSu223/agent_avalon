\section{Conclusion}

We asked whether the loop that keeps agent memory small destroys the content memory exists for, and
built an instrument in which prior-fit is graded, measured on the compactor itself, and --- in
\textsc{E-Shift} --- manipulated while the target sentence and its value are held exactly fixed, with
the protocol frozen on a development split beforehand. We found no support for the prediction: the
causal manipulation excludes a benefit above \ConeUpper{}~pp and the graded dose--response is centred
on zero, while the choice of operator moves retention by twenty points or more. Strong compactors do
not quietly rewrite constraints into plausible defaults; under a binding budget they delete. We are
equally explicit about where the instrument falls short, and release everything so that both the
regime boundaries and those limitations can be pushed on directly.
