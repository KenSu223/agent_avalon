\section{What this means for memory design}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The bottleneck is where the risk lives.} The clearest practical finding is negative in a
useful way: at the word budgets a strong compactor normally operates under, it loses essentially
nothing. Retention at $k{=}10$ is \RetentionBThreeHundred{} at a 300-word budget and
\RetentionBSixHundred{} at 600. Degradation only appears once the budget is genuinely binding. A
system that reports a comfortable compression ratio is not thereby safe; what matters is whether the
budget forces a choice, and only then does \emph{which} content is chosen become a question about
the model's expectations.

\paragraph{Loss is deletion, not corruption.} Across \NCodedCases{} coded item-checkpoints, only
\ChangedCount{} (\ChangedRate{}) are changes to a different value. The failure mode practitioners
worry about --- a constraint quietly rewritten into a plausible default --- is not what we observe
in a strong compactor. What we observe is that the item is simply gone. This matters for mitigation:
checks that compare a stored value against an expected range will not fire, because there is nothing
left to compare. Detecting this failure requires knowing what \emph{used to be} in memory, which is
exactly what rolling rewrite destroys.

\paragraph{Keep the original, or keep a pointer to it.} The operator lattice makes the design
implication concrete. Append-only with oracle retrieval is a ceiling by construction; verbatim
selection (\textsc{Copy-LLM}) removes the rewriting channel at the cost of compression; and pinning
specific lines works as a mechanism, because our implementation \emph{constructs} the stored state
from the pinned text rather than asking the model to preserve it. Asking is not enough: the
anti-drift wording shipped in production memory-consolidation prompts is a request, and a request is
satisfied or not at the model's discretion.

\paragraph{Personalisation.} If what survives compaction is what the model already expected, then
the parts of a user profile that carry information --- the unusual date format, the unusual unit, the
unusual constraint --- are the parts most at risk, and the surviving profile drifts toward the
profile of an average user. We report this as an application rather than a claim about people: the
eight profiles are controlled test cases, and generalisation is over preference families.
