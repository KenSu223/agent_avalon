\section{Compaction as iterated learning}
\label{sec:background}

Let $M_t$ be the memory after $t$ compactions and $O_t$ the session that just ended. Every system we
surveyed implements some $M_{t+1} = C(M_t, O_t)$ where $C$ is a language model under a length
budget. For a single memory item $m$ --- a fact, a preference, a lesson --- and sessions that do not
mention $m$ again, the item's representation $r_{t+1}$ depends only on $r_t$ and the fixed
surrounding context. That is a Markov chain over representations, and it is the object iterated
learning studies.

Classical results say that a chain of Bayesian learners who \emph{sample} from the posterior
converges to the prior, while learners who take the \emph{MAP} hypothesis amplify the prior's mode,
with the amplification governed by the bottleneck rather than by the strength of the prior
\citep{griffiths2007language,kirby2007innateness}. We deliberately do \emph{not} claim either result
holds for an LLM summarisation chain. Two things stand in the way. First, an LLM's in-context
inference need not be posterior sampling even at temperature one \citep{cui2025sharper}. Second, and
more decisively, deletion is close to absorbing in a real memory system: once an item is gone it is
not usually regenerated, so the literal stationary distribution of the chain is ``everything is
forgotten'', which is true but uninformative.

What survives the correction is the useful part. We therefore state the claim about
\emph{selection and replacement} rather than about stationarity:

\begin{quote}
Survival of an item through compaction falls with its surprise under the compactor's prior, and when
an item is not retained verbatim its replacement is drawn toward higher-prior values.
\end{quote}

Formally we model the chain with states $\{R_1,\dots,R_5,\textsc{other},\textsc{vague},
\textsc{absent}\}$, where $R_i$ is the $i$-th ranked value of that item's family under the
\emph{measured} prior, $\textsc{other}$ is any value outside the family's candidate set,
$\textsc{vague}$ is a non-specific answer, and $\textsc{absent}$ is deletion. We estimate the kernel
$K$ from a single step, predict $p_0K^k$ on held-out trajectories, and report the distribution
conditional on survival.

Drift requires a bottleneck. If $C$ copied its input verbatim the chain would not move. Every
operator we study therefore has an explicit budget, and we verify empirically that the budget
removes material content before interpreting anything (\S\ref{sec:adequacy}).
