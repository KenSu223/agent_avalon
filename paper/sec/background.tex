\section{Compaction as iterated learning}
\label{sec:background}

Let $M_t$ be the memory after $t$ compactions and $O_t$ the session that just ended. Every system we
surveyed implements some $M_{t+1} = C(M_t, O_t)$ where $C$ is a language model under a length
budget. For a single memory item $m$ --- a fact, a preference, a lesson --- and sessions that do not
mention $m$ again, the item's representation $r_{t+1}$ depends only on $r_t$ and the fixed
surrounding context. That is a Markov chain over representations, and it is the object iterated
learning studies.

Classical results say that a chain of Bayesian learners who \emph{sample} from the posterior
converges to the prior, while \emph{MAP} learners amplify its mode
\citep{griffiths2007language,kirby2007innateness}. We deliberately do \emph{not} claim either holds
for an LLM summarisation chain: in-context inference need not be posterior sampling even at
temperature one \citep{cui2025sharper}, and, more decisively, deletion is close to absorbing in a real
memory system, so the literal stationary distribution is ``everything is forgotten'' --- true but
uninformative. We therefore state the \emph{hypothesis under test} about selection and replacement
rather than about stationarity:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{H.} Survival of an item through compaction falls with its surprise under the compactor's
prior, and when an item is not retained verbatim its replacement is drawn toward higher-prior values.
\end{quote}

\noindent This is the proposition the rest of the paper tests. We report in \S\ref{sec:results}
that it does not hold for the compactors we study, and what does hold instead.

Formally we model the chain over states $\{R_1,\dots,R_5,\textsc{other},\textsc{vague},
\textsc{absent}\}$, with $R_i$ the $i$-th ranked value of the item's family under the \emph{measured}
prior (Appendix~\ref{app:kernel}). Drift requires a bottleneck: if $C$ copied its input verbatim the
chain would not move, so every operator has an explicit budget and we verify that the budget removes
material content before interpreting anything (\S\ref{sec:adequacy}).
